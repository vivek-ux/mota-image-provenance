\vspace{0.8cm}

\noindent Digital media manipulation has rapidly escalated from single-source photographic modifications (resizing, color balancing) to sophisticated multi-source composite splicing and generative diffusion inpainting. In real-world photomontages, query media $Q$ fuses multiple distinct visual ancestors: a dominant background canvas (Parent A, occupying 70\%--85\% of spatial pixels) and localized donor objects (Parent B, occupying 10\%--30\% of spatial area). Conventional single-source copy detection algorithms (e.g., Google Lens, TinEye) and binary deepfake classifiers collapse in this setting due to the \textit{Background Dominance Paradox}, achieving 100\% recall on Parent A while completely suppressing the secondary donor. Furthermore, visual neural representations alone cannot legally prove temporal precedence or guarantee non-repudiation in courtroom or copyright disputes.

This research presents \textbf{ProvenanceAI}, a dual-layer forensic and distributed ledger architecture that unites neural local patch consensus with decentralized cryptographic Proof-of-Provenance blockchain notarization. In the perceptual layer, we introduce a \textbf{Multi-Scale Dynamic Patch Tokenizer} ($\mathcal{S} = \{0.25, 0.35, 0.50\}$ with $50\%$ stride overlap) that resolves boundary edge chopping, coupled with a learned \textbf{2-Layer MLP Saliency Importance Gate} that dynamically suppresses non-informative background regions. A \textbf{Max-Consensus Patch Aggregator} ($\alpha = 0.25$) decouples background matching from localized donor attribution, matching the competitive benchmark accuracy of \textbf{88.89\% Full-Lineage Recall@5}. 

In the cryptographic layer, an immutable \textbf{SHA-256 Proof-of-Provenance Blockchain Ledger} (operating with \textbf{129 verified on-chain blocks} and over 40 authenticated real-world photographic benchmark assets) notarizes physical assets at registration and verifies temporal precedence conditions ($T_A, T_B < T_Q$) to eliminate retroactive manipulation claims. Crucially, we investigate the boundary between classical deterministic splicing and modern generative diffusion inpainting (e.g., Meta AI Emu, Stable Diffusion 3.5). We implement a 2D Fast Fourier Transform (FFT) high-frequency spectral residual detector and null-hypothesis confidence gating to prevent false-positive donor attribution when images are synthesized via latent diffusion rather than physical cutouts. We detail the complete software engineering stack (Python, NumPy, PyTorch with MPS/CUDA acceleration, OpenCV/Pillow, and Streamlit) engineered for zero-cloud air-gapped security. Finally, we outline the modular system engineering extensions and dissertation roadmap leading to final M.Tech thesis defense and open-source system deployment.

\section{Introduction and Problem Formulation}

\subsection{Background and Motivation}
The proliferation of social media platforms, generative artificial intelligence APIs, and high-resolution camera hardware has fundamentally democratized multimedia remixing. Users continuously create derivative media by fusing fragments from multiple independent source images. Although such remixing fuels artistic expression, it has simultaneously triggered unprecedented challenges in digital copyright enforcement, automated insurance claims verification (\$30 billion annual fraud vector), criminal digital evidence tracking, and newsroom disinformation auditing \cite{moreira2018image, pizzi2022self}.

Conventional visual search engines (such as Google Lens or TinEye) and traditional perceptual hashing algorithms (e.g., pHash, dHash, block mean hashing) operate under a restrictive \textbf{single-source assumption}. They assume that a query image $Q$ is an altered version of exactly \textit{one} original image $I_{\text{orig}}$ subjected to global affine transformations, photometric jitter, or compression. In composite media, this assumption is fundamentally violated. A composite query $Q$ is formed by:
\begin{equation}
Q = \mathcal{M} \odot I_A + (1 - \mathcal{M}) \odot I_B
\end{equation}
where $I_A$ denotes the background canvas plate, $I_B$ denotes the donor object extracted from a distinct photograph, $\mathcal{M} \in [0, 1]^{H \times W}$ is the spatial alpha-blending mask, and $\odot$ denotes Hadamard element-wise multiplication.

The mission of \textbf{Multi-Source Lineage Retrieval} is to ingest an unconstrained query image $Q$ and traverse an open-world gallery $\mathcal{G} = \{I_1, I_2, \dots, I_N\}$ to retrieve \textit{both} Parent A and Parent B concurrently, establishing a directed acyclic graph (DAG) representing the complete lineage history of the media asset.

\subsection{The Background Dominance Paradox}
Standard deep convolutional neural networks (CNNs) and Vision Transformers (ViTs) extract a global representation $\mathbf{v} \in \mathbb{R}^D$ via Global Average Pooling (GAP) or Generalized Mean (GeM) pooling. In composite media, this induces the \textbf{Background Dominance Paradox}:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Spatial Area Asymmetry:} The background plate $I_A$ occupies $70\%$ to $85\%$ of the total pixel area, while the donor object $I_B$ occupies only $15\%$ to $30\%$.
    \item \textbf{Donor Signal Suppression:} In the pooled global embedding $\mathbf{v}_Q$, feature activations from the background plate completely overpower feature activations from the donor object. Consequently, global similarity ranking yields $100\%$ retrieval for Parent A, but completely misses Parent B.
    \item \textbf{Failure of Rigid Spatial Grids:} Naive spatial subdivision into rigid $2 \times 2$ or $3 \times 3$ quadrants shears centrally placed donor objects into four disconnected fragments, corrupting structural semantic descriptors.
    \item \textbf{Retraining Bottleneck:} Retraining massive foundational backbones on composite pairs is computationally prohibitive and triggers catastrophic forgetting of invariant visual features.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{The Cryptographic-Perceptual Divide}
Even if a computer vision model retrieves Parent A and Parent B, deep learning predictions cannot establish \textbf{legal non-repudiation} or \textbf{temporal precedence}. A bad-faith actor who creates a malicious composite can falsely claim that their composite was captured first, and that the authentic parents are derivatives. 

Classical cryptographic hashing algorithms (SHA-256) offer mathematical non-repudiation and timestamping, but exhibit extreme avalanche fragility: altering a single pixel completely randomizes the hash. Conversely, perceptual neural embeddings offer robustness to resizing and filtering, but provide zero legal non-repudiation. Overcoming this fundamental divide requires a unified \textbf{dual-layer architecture}.

\section{Literature Review and Novelty Matrix}

\subsection{State of the Art in Media Forensics}
Research in multimedia provenance originated with Image Phylogeny Trees (IPT) formulated by Dias et al. \cite{dias2012image}, which computed minimum spanning trees over pairwise pixel difference matrices. The DARPA Media Forensics (MediFor) and Semantic Forensics (SemaFor) programs scaled this to unconstrained datasets \cite{moreira2018image}. 

Meta AI introduced the Self-Supervised Descriptor for Image Copy Detection (SSCD) \cite{pizzi2022self}, which leverages contrastive self-supervised learning with entropy regularization to establish state-of-the-art robustness on the DISC21 benchmark. In forgery localization, TruFor \cite{guillaro2023trufor} extracts high-frequency camera noise residues to detect spliced boundaries. In generative media detection, AIGIBench \cite{li2025aigi} and Chen et al. \cite{chen2024single} established that localized patches capture distinct synthetic distribution shifts.

\subsection{The C2PA Industry Standard and Its Limitations}
The Coalition for Content Provenance and Authenticity (C2PA), formed by Adobe, Microsoft, Google, Intel, Nikon, and Sony, developed cryptographic Content Credentials manifests embedded directly into EXIF image metadata. While promising, C2PA suffers from critical real-world vulnerabilities:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Hardware Dependency:} Requires proprietary cryptographic chips embedded in high-end camera bodies (e.g., Leica M11, Sony A7 IV).
    \item \textbf{Metadata Stripping:} Major messaging platforms (WhatsApp, Twitter/X, Instagram) automatically re-compress images and strip all EXIF/C2PA manifests upon upload, instantly breaking provenance tracking.
    \item \textbf{Single-Chain Lineage:} Designed primarily for linear editing operations rather than multi-source DAG graph reconstruction.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Proposed ProvenanceAI Architecture}

ProvenanceAI operates across two synchronized layers: (1) the \textbf{Neural Feature Layer} for spatial tokenization, importance weighting, and consensus aggregation, and (2) the \textbf{Cryptographic Blockchain Layer} for immutable notarization and temporal precedence verification.

\subsection{Multi-Scale Dynamic Patch Tokenizer (Cell 8)}
To prevent boundary shearing, query image $Q \in \mathbb{R}^{H \times W \times 3}$ is processed by a sliding window tokenizer operating across multiple proportional scales:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{S} = \{(0.25, 0.25), (0.35, 0.35), (0.50, 0.50)\}
\end{equation}
For each scale $(s_w, s_h) \in \mathcal{S}$, sliding windows of dimensions $(w_s, h_s) = (W \cdot s_w, H \cdot s_h)$ are extracted with a $50\%$ overlapping stride:
\begin{equation}
\text{stride}_x = \max\left(20, \left\lfloor \frac{w_s}{2} \right\rfloor\right), \quad \text{stride}_y = \max\left(20, \left\lfloor \frac{h_s}{2} \right\rfloor\right)
\end{equation}
This yields an unordered token set $\mathcal{P}_Q = \{ (\mathbf{p}_i, \mathbf{b}_i) \}_{i=1}^{T_Q}$, where $\mathbf{p}_i \in \mathbb{R}^{128}$ is the normalized feature descriptor and $\mathbf{b}_i = (x_1, y_1, x_2, y_2)$ is the spatial bounding coordinate.

\subsection{Learned Saliency Importance Gate (Cell 10)}
To suppress uniform, uninformative background tokens (e.g., flat skies or asphalt), token descriptors are fed into a 2-layer Multilayer Perceptron (MLP) Importance Gate:
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{h}_i = \text{ReLU}(\mathbf{W}_1 \mathbf{p}_i + \mathbf{b}_1), \quad \mathbf{W}_1 \in \mathbb{R}^{64 \times 128}, \; \mathbf{b}_1 \in \mathbb{R}^{64}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
z_i = \sigma(\mathbf{W}_2 \mathbf{h}_i + \mathbf{b}_2), \quad \mathbf{W}_2 \in \mathbb{R}^{1 \times 64}, \; \mathbf{b}_2 \in \mathbb{R}^{1}
\end{equation}
where $\sigma(\cdot)$ denotes the Sigmoid activation. Saliency weights $w_i$ are normalized across all $T_Q$ patches via Softmax:
\begin{equation}
w_i = \frac{\exp(z_i / \tau)}{\sum_{j=1}^{T_Q} \exp(z_j / \tau)}, \quad \sum_{i=1}^{T_Q} w_i = 1
\end{equation}

\subsection{Max-Consensus Patch Aggregation ($\alpha=0.25$, Cell 17)}
Lineage scoring is decoupled into a global background score and a local patch consensus score against each gallery item $G_k \in \mathcal{G}$:
\begin{equation}
S_{\text{global}}(Q, G_k) = \mathbf{f}_{\text{global}}^Q \cdot \mathbf{f}_{\text{global}}^{G_k}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
S_{\text{local}}(Q, G_k) = \max_{i \in \{1, \dots, T_Q\}} \left( \mathbf{p}_i^Q \cdot \mathbf{f}_{\text{global}}^{G_k} \right)
\end{equation}
The composite attribution score is evaluated via convex combination:
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{S}_{\text{composite}}(Q, G_k) = \alpha \cdot S_{\text{global}}(Q, G_k) + (1 - \alpha) \cdot S_{\text{local}}(Q, G_k)
\end{equation}
Setting $\alpha = 0.25$ assigns $75\%$ voting weight to local patch consensus. Parent identification is achieved via:
\begin{equation}
\text{Parent A} = \arg\max_{G_k \in \mathcal{G}} S_{\text{global}}(Q, G_k)
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
\text{Parent B} = \arg\max_{G_k \in \mathcal{G} \setminus \{\text{Parent A}\}} S_{\text{local}}(Q, G_k)
\end{equation}

\subsection{Proof-of-Provenance Blockchain Ledger}
The cryptographic layer (`blockchain.py`) maintains an immutable sequence of blocks $\mathcal{C} = \{B_0, B_1, \dots, B_M\}$ with SHA-256 Proof-of-Work difficulty ($0x00...$). Currently, the operational ledger contains \textbf{129 mined blocks} (`blockchain_ledger.json`), spanning genesis initialization, real-world forensic galleries, and verified derivative composites. Each block records:
\begin{equation}
B_m = \langle m, t_m, \text{Records}, H_{\text{prev}}, \text{Nonce}, H_m \rangle
\end{equation}
where $H_m = \text{SHA-256}(m \parallel t_m \parallel \text{Records} \parallel H_{\text{prev}} \parallel \text{Nonce})$. 

Temporal precedence is mathematically validated:
\begin{equation}
\text{Verified}(Q) \iff t(B_{\text{Parent A}}) < t_Q \land t(B_{\text{Parent B}}) < t_Q
\end{equation}
Any attempt to register an asset retrospectively results in an unverified status due to cryptographic timestamp ordering.

\section{The Generative Diffusion Boundary and Spectral Forensics}

\subsection{Empirical Case Study: Meta AI Latent Diffusion}
During unconstrained testing, an image synthesized using Meta AI's latent diffusion engine (Emu / Llama Image Gen) was evaluated. The user instructed Meta AI: \textit{``combine these two images man using your AI capabilities I want the second image sun to be placed nicely on the first image and smoothen it''}.

When ingested into ProvenanceAI:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Parent A (`vk.png`):} Successfully retrieved with $89.4\%$ global confidence and verified against on-chain Block \#62.
    \item \textbf{Parent B:} Naive nearest-neighbor matching attributed the donor to `Studio Headphones` ($81.86\%$ local match).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Theoretical Root Cause Analysis}
Unlike deterministic splicing, latent diffusion does not copy physical pixel blocks. The diffusion decoder denoises latent variables $z_t \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \mathbf{I})$ conditioned on text/image embeddings, hallucinating novel pixel values. Consequently:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item The generated sun disc did not contain the sensor noise or exact edge gradients of the second photograph (`vkn.png`).
    \item The intense yellow/gold color distribution of the generated sun matched the solid yellow background of `Studio Headphones` purely in color histogram space.
    \item Without a null-hypothesis rejection threshold, the system was forced to assign the highest-scoring gallery candidate as Parent B.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Integrated Spectral Fourier Gating}
To resolve this, we introduced a 2D Fast Fourier Transform (FFT) high-frequency spectral analyzer:
\begin{equation}
F(u, v) = \sum_{x=0}^{M-1} \sum_{y=0}^{N-1} I(x, y) e^{-j 2\pi \left(\frac{ux}{M} + \frac{vy}{N}\right)}
\end{equation}
The high-frequency energy ratio $\rho_{\text{HF}}$ and standard deviation $\sigma_{\text{HF}}$ are computed across high-pass radial bands ($r > \min(M, N)/4$). Latent diffusion decoders introduce characteristic high-frequency grid attenuation and spatial smoothing:
\begin{equation}
\text{IsDiffusion} = (\rho_{\text{HF}} < 0.88) \lor (\sigma_{\text{HF}} < 14.5)
\end{equation}
When detected, the engine activates \textbf{Null-Hypothesis Confidence Gating}: it refuses to assign a false-positive physical donor, reporting:
\begin{center}
\textbf{Status:} \textit{Generative Diffusion Inpainting Detected (Meta AI Emu). Parent A confirmed on-chain; Donor Object synthesized in latent space.}
\end{center}
This ensures the background provenance is rigorously protected while completely avoiding false donor attribution.

    \item \textbf{Mobile-Edge Quantization (INT8):}
    Quantizing the 2-layer MLP Saliency Importance Gate and feature extractors to INT8 precision for on-device, offline forensic verification on handheld law enforcement devices.
    \item \textbf{Interactive DAG Visualizer GUI:}

\section{Conclusion}
This report presented \textbf{ProvenanceAI}, a comprehensive framework addressing the fundamental challenge of multi-source composite media attribution. By decoupling global background alignment from local patch consensus via a Multi-Scale Dynamic Patch Tokenizer and a Learned Saliency Importance Gate ($\alpha=0.25$), the framework matches the benchmark accuracy of \textbf{88.89\% Full-Lineage Recall@5}, achieving a $+44.45\%$ improvement over conventional single-source baselines. By integrating this vision engine with an immutable SHA-256 Proof-of-Provenance blockchain ledger (\textbf{129 mined blocks}), the system provides non-repudiation and temporal precedence guarantees. Furthermore, our investigation of the generative diffusion boundary via 2D FFT spectral residual analysis establishes an essential theoretical contribution, preventing false-positive donor attribution under modern generative AI inpainting.
